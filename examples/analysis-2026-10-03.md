# Analysis note: global news snapshot, 3 Oct 2026

_Author: Raul de la Hera_

> Second worked example with OSINT-AI, run on public RSS news. Public data only. This one goes
> past the overview and pulls on a single thread to show how I'd actually use the tool as an
> analyst. Everything factual below is attributed to the outlet that reported it.

## 1. Scope and data

I pulled 179 articles on 3 October 2026 from the same five public RSS feeds: NYT World (57),
The Guardian World (45), BBC World (42), Al Jazeera (25) and NPR World (10). The pipeline is
unchanged from the first run: RSS ingest, spaCy NER for people, organisations and places, an
entity co-occurrence graph, VADER sentiment per article, and a local retrieval layer on top.

Same health warning as last time. One day, English language, five Western outlets. Read this as
the shape of their agenda for the day and nothing wider.

## 2. The shape of the day

The graph came out at 489 nodes and 1722 edges. The first thing that jumped out is how flat the
top of the ranking is. On 11 September the strongest link was Russia-Ukraine at 26 co-occurrences
and it towered over everything. Today the heaviest links top out at 4: Russia-Kyiv, Russia-Ukraine,
US-Trump, Israeli-Israel, Britain-UK, Tel Aviv-Dubai. No single story owns the day. The agenda is
split across five or six medium-sized ones, which is a real difference in the news picture and not
a quirk of the method.

"US" is still the highest-centrality node (0.186, degree 91), the same bridging-hub pattern as
before. Below it the clusters are:

- An aviation security story around FlyDubai, Tel Aviv and Dubai.
- Brazil's presidential election (Brazil, Lula, Bolsonaro, and a US angle).
- Russia and Ukraine, centred on Kyiv.
- A US death-penalty story out of Tennessee.
- Smaller threads: a UK-Iran strand, France, India, and a light Oktoberfest item.

## 3. The thread I pulled: FlyDubai flight 1073

This is the cluster I'd chase first, and it's a good example of why the graph is useful for triage.
Tel Aviv and FlyDubai light up together, and when I open the articles behind them the corpus has
roughly a dozen pieces on one event: an attack in the cockpit of FlyDubai flight 1073.

What the headlines actually support, by outlet:

- The NYT reports Emirates describing it as a "terrorist" cockpit attack, says a co-pilot used a
  crash ax, and runs follow-ups on pilot background checks and on why four pilots were aboard.
- The Guardian covers an Indian pilot stabbed in the attack and describes him being hailed as a
  hero, and runs a piece openly weighing the motive ("suicide by plane or terrorism?").
- NPR has passenger accounts of the chaos on the flight.

So three independent outlets corroborate the core event, which raises my confidence that something
real happened on that flight. What they do not settle is the motive. One outlet is already flagging
that the "terrorist" label is contested, so I'd hold that part as unconfirmed. If I were taking this
further, the next step leaves OSINT-AI entirely: go to the primary sources, the Emirates and FlyDubai
statements and any aviation-authority filing, and check the claims against those before repeating any
of them. The tool got me to the thread fast. It can't tell me whether the "terrorist" framing holds.

## 4. The other clusters, quickly

- **Brazil.** Coverage clusters on the 2026 presidential election, Lula against Bolsonaro's son,
  with a US angle that several outlets push: a BBC piece on Brazil accusing the US of election
  interference, and an NYT line on the US suspending consular services on the eve of the vote.
- **Russia-Ukraine.** Kyiv is taking strikes, with the BBC and Al Jazeera both reporting Russia
  hitting a second bridge, and an NYT line on Ukraine's first combat use of its own ballistic missile.
- **Tennessee.** A botched execution of Christa Pike and the resignation of a prison official,
  carried across BBC, Guardian and Al Jazeera.

## 5. Sentiment

Mean compound sentiment is **−0.345**, with 124 articles negative, 45 positive and 10 neutral, so
about 69% read negative. That's a touch heavier than the −0.267 on 11 September, which fits a day
led by a cockpit attack, strikes on Kyiv and a botched execution. Same caveat as before: VADER is
scoring the wording of the coverage and working off short text, so I'd trust the direction of the
change more than the exact figure.

## 6. Where the method slips

The same weaknesses show up, plus a new one worth noting.

- **Entity resolution.** "Israel" and "Israeli" are separate nodes again, as are "UK", "Britain"
  and "British", and "US" and "U.S." (U.S. alone is mentioned six times as its own place). An
  alias pass is still the highest-value fix.
- **NER noise.** "AI" got tagged as an organisation this time and even surfaced in the place list,
  which is nonsense. A stop-list clears it.
- **Feed self-reference.** "Guardian" lands as a high-centrality node, degree 29. That's the
  outlet's own name leaking in from its feed metadata, not a real actor in the news. Worth
  stripping the source names before building the graph.
- **Co-occurrence is not a relationship.** Tel Aviv and Dubai sit together because of one aviation
  story, not because of a standing link between the cities. The edge is a pointer to go read, and
  that's all it is.

## 7. Takeaway

Two runs in, the pattern holds. The tool is good at one job: take a pile of raw RSS and show me the
shape of the day in a couple of minutes, including when that shape is a single dominant story
(11 September) or a fragmented field (today). From there the real work is human. I used the graph to
find the FlyDubai thread, the corpus to confirm three outlets were carrying it, and my own judgement
to stop short of the motive, which the headlines can't settle. The machine gets me the breadth in
minutes; the checking is still mine to do.
