# Analysis note: global news snapshot, 11 Sep 2026

_Author: Raul de la Hera_

> Example investigation produced with OSINT-AI on public RSS news. Method-focused and
> deliberately cautious about what the data can and cannot support. Public data only.

## 1. Scope and data

- **Corpus:** 160 articles collected on 11 September 2026 from five public news RSS feeds:
  NYT World (55), The Guardian World (45), Al Jazeera (25), BBC World (24), NPR World (10).
- **Pipeline:** RSS ingest, spaCy NER (people, organisations, places), entity co-occurrence
  graph, VADER sentiment per article, Nominatim geocoding of places, and local retrieval (RAG)
  for question-answering.
- **Caveat up front:** this is a single-day, English-language, Western-outlet snapshot. It
  reflects what these five newsrooms chose to cover, not the world. Read everything below as a
  view of the news agenda, not of ground truth.

## 2. What the graph shows

Two entities are linked when they appear in the same article. With no manual tagging, three
clusters dominate.

1. **Russia and Ukraine.** The strongest link in the whole corpus (Russia-Ukraine, 26
   co-occurrences), backed by Europe-Ukraine (14) and Patriot-Ukraine (12). The Patriot link
   is the air-defence and weapons-supply storyline specifically, not the war in general.
2. **Middle East and Red Sea.** A tight cluster around Iran: Iran-US (13), Iran-Yemen (12),
   Iran-Red Sea (9), with an Israel/Israeli sub-group. This tracks the maritime-security and
   regional-tension coverage.
3. **US domestic politics.** Trump-US (11), JD Vance-US (10), Republicans-US (10).

One node, "US", connects to all three clusters: Ukraine aid, Iran, and domestic politics. It
is the highest-centrality node in the graph. That is exactly what you would expect for a global
news corpus, so it doubles as a sanity check that the method finds the obvious hub.

## 3. Sentiment

Mean article sentiment (VADER compound) is **−0.267**. The split is 99 negative, 48 positive,
13 neutral, so 62% of articles read as negative.

That is not surprising given how much of the agenda is conflict. One thing to keep in mind:
VADER scores the language of the coverage, not the events, and it is working off short headline
and summary text. Treat it as a rough mood gauge that is useful for tracking change over time,
not as an absolute number.

## 4. Geography

Geocoded place mentions cluster over Ukraine and Eastern Europe, the Gulf and Red Sea, and
Washington. It is the same three stories again, this time laid out on a map. The map earns its
place as a quick "where is the attention today" view, not much more.

## 5. What I would fix next

- **Entity resolution is naive.** "Israel", "Israeli" and "Israelis" are three separate nodes.
  So are "UK" and "British", and "US" and "U.S.". This inflates the node count and splits what
  should be one entity. An alias/normalisation pass is the single highest-value improvement.
- **NER errors.** "AI" got tagged as a place (GPE), and one feed slug
  ("anthony-albanese-september-11") leaked in as a PERSON. A short clean-up and stop-list would
  drop these.
- **Co-occurrence is not a relationship.** Two entities sharing an article does not mean there
  is a real-world link. It is an attention signal, a place for a human to start, not a
  conclusion.
- **Source bias.** Five Western, English-language outlets. Adding non-Western and non-English
  feeds would change the picture, and it is the honest way to cut the bias.

## 6. Takeaway

No single line here is the finding. The point is that going from raw RSS to three coherent
themes, a mood reading and a map took one automated pass. The right use is triage: see the
shape of the day's information space fast, spot the hubs and the bridges, then hand the
interesting threads to a person to check against primary sources. Machine for breadth, human
for the judgement.
